### 4.3 RQ3: Why does whitening hurt?

**The outcome is carried by the high-variance directions in every cell.** The signal–variance alignment exceeds 1 in {{sva_share_gt1|.0%}} of the {{n_cells}} cells, with a median of {{sva_median|.1f}}: the principal components that correlate with the outcome have, on average, several times the mean eigenvalue. Learner ability vectors have a strong common factor (a learner who is strong on one skill tends to be strong on others), and that factor is what predicts a future or held-out performance.

**The path from Mahalanobis to Euclidean is monotone.** Figure 6a moves from the sample Mahalanobis distance (α = 0) to the Euclidean distance (α = 1) through fixed shrinkage weights. At every training size, each step towards the Euclidean distance improves the mean R². The best point of the path is the Euclidean end in {{alpha_star_share_euclid|.0%}} of the cells. Shrinkage estimators help only because they move the metric towards the identity, and their data-driven weight stops far too early for retrieval: it is chosen to estimate the covariance well, which is a different goal.

**The loss from whitening tracks the alignment.** Across cells, the difference between the Ledoit–Wolf Mahalanobis distance and the Euclidean distance has a Spearman correlation of {{mechanism/d_maha~sva/0|.2f}} [{{mechanism/d_maha~sva/1|.2f}}, {{mechanism/d_maha~sva/2|.2f}}] with the signal–variance alignment (Figure 6c), stronger than with any label-free diagnostic (Table A15; n/d: {{mechanism/d_maha~log_n_over_d/0|.2f}}). Splitting the cells into terciles of alignment orders the three paths in Figure 6b as the argument of Section 3.7 predicts. We found no cell with alignment below 1, so the complementary prediction, that whitening helps when the outcome sits in low-variance directions, remains untested on these data.

![Figure 6. From Mahalanobis to Euclidean, and the role of the signal–variance alignment.](../results/figures/fig6_alpha_mechanism.png)

*Figure 6. (a) Mean R² difference to the Euclidean distance along the path M = ((1 − α) S + α I)⁻¹, by training size. (b) The same path for terciles of the signal–variance alignment. (c) Difference between the Ledoit–Wolf Mahalanobis distance and the Euclidean distance in each cell against the alignment.*

**What cosine adds to the Euclidean distance.** The advantage of cosine over Euclidean is largest where the vectors are sparse: its Spearman correlation with the feature density is {{mechanism/d_cos~density/0|.2f}}, with the Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage weight {{mechanism/d_cos~lw_alpha/0|.2f}}, and with the variance share of the first component {{mechanism/d_cos~top_eig_share/0|.2f}}. In a sparse standardised vector the norm reflects mainly how many skills the learner has practised, because unpractised skills sit at the prior. The Euclidean distance matches learners on that amount of practice; the cosine discards it and keeps the direction. Cosine is also the least hub-prone of the effective measures (mean skewness of the neighbour-occurrence distribution {{hub/cosine|.2f}}, against {{hub/euclid|.2f}} for Euclidean and {{hub/maha_lw|.2f}} for the Ledoit–Wolf Mahalanobis distance; Table A6).

**How much of the profile is used?** On the d = 50 views we also retrieved neighbours on a single number, the learner's mean standardised feature. At n = 100 this one-dimensional match reaches an R² of {{ability_r2/ability_1d/100|.3f}}, level with cosine ({{ability_r2/cosine/100|.3f}}); at n = 500 it reaches {{ability_r2/ability_1d/500|.3f}} against {{ability_r2/cosine/500|.3f}} for cosine. With about 100 learners, good retrieval for an overall outcome is mostly matching on general ability, and the rest of the profile begins to contribute as the cohort grows. Section 4.6 examines an outcome for which general ability is not enough.

The rule describes the data, but with eight sources it does not transfer reliably, and we do not recommend it over the fixed choice.

**Selection inside the cohort helps only from about 200 learners.** Picking the label-free measure with the best leave-one-out R² among the training learners has a mean regret of {{selector/internal LOO among label-free measures|.3f}}. ### 4.5 Protocol effects

Figure 8 shows the three protocol variants.

**Transductive preprocessing.** Estimating the scaling and the covariance on training and test learners together changes the R² of the Ledoit–Wolf Mahalanobis distance by {{transductive/50/0|+.3f}} at n = 50 and by {{transductive/500/0|+.3f}} at n = 500 (Table A9). The change is negative here because a better covariance estimate whitens more completely, which is harmful on these data; on data where whitening helps, the same shortcut would inflate the score. The comparison with the Euclidean distance changes sign in {{transductive_flip_share|.1%}} of the cells, so the shortcut does not alter our conclusions. A shift of this size at n = 50 nevertheless makes results obtained under the two protocols incomparable.

**Random instead of temporal outcome split.** Drawing the outcome interactions at random instead of from the end of the sequence raises the R² of cosine retrieval by {{random_split/inflation_cosine|+.3f}} and of ridge regression by {{random_split/inflation_ridge|+.3f}} on average over {{random_split/cells}} cells of three sources (Table A10), a relative inflation of {{random_split/rel_inflation_ridge|.0%}} for ridge. The ranking of the methods is largely preserved (mean Spearman correlation {{random_split/rank_spearman|.2f}}; the same best measure in {{random_split/same_best|.0%}} of the cells). A random split therefore overstates how well a learner's future can be predicted while leaving the comparison between measures mostly intact.

**Training on other modules.** When the OULAD training cohort comes from the other five modules, the AUC falls by {{lomo/auc_gap_mean|.3f}} on average over methods and training sizes; for logistic regression at n = 500 it falls from {{lomo/auc_within_ridge_500|.3f}} to {{lomo/auc_lomo_ridge_500|.3f}} (Table A11), in line with the drop reported by Lim (2026) on the full data. The Brier skill falls to zero or below for every method: the ranking of learners transfers in part, the calibrated risk does not. The ordering of the similarity measures is also less stable under this shift (mean Spearman correlation between the two rankings {{lomo/rank_spearman|.2f}}).
