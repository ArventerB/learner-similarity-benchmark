Similar-learner retrieval rests on a similarity measure that is rarely examined. In a benchmark of {{n_measures}} measures on {{n_views}} views from eight public sources, with cohorts of 50 to 500 learners, the cosine similarity on standardised ability vectors was the best or co-best measure at every size, whitening in any form removed most of the predictive signal, and learned metrics reached but did not exceed cosine. A single diagnostic, the alignment between the outcome and the high-variance directions of the ability vector, explains the failure of whitening: the outcome is carried by the common factor that whitening suppresses. No selection rule learned on other sources improved on always using cosine, and selecting inside the cohort helped only from about 200 learners.

For small cohorts, the practical message is to standardise carefully, use cosine, avoid the Mahalanobis distance unless the alignment diagnostic says otherwise, and reserve learned metrics for larger data. Future work should test denser, model-based mastery vectors, item-level outcomes, nonlinear metrics, sample sizes beyond 500, and whether the neighbours that score best here also serve a downstream tutor or language model best.
